# SECOND LINE

### *In New Orleans, every funeral gets a parade.*

**Format:** 8 × 60-minute hour-long crime drama · **Rating:** TV-MA
**Setting:** New Orleans, Louisiana — the 7th Ward, the Lower Ninth, Gentilly, St. Claude Avenue, Bourbon Street, and the shadow under the Claiborne overpass.
**Tone:** *Snowfall*'s rise-and-fall, *The Wire*'s system-level truth, and the heat, brass and humidity only New Orleans has.

---

## THE PITCH

Dashon "Locs" Batiste, Terrell "T-Bone" Guidry, Andre "Dre Fly" Marigny and Kendrick "Kebo" LeBlanc grew up on the same block in the 7th Ward. They've been brothers since Pampers. Now they're 21, broke, and watching the city sell itself to tourists one daiquiri at a time.

When a stray bullet at a Sunday second line hits Locs's 15-year-old cousin, the friends decide the only way to protect their own is to *become* the power on the block. A Houston cocaine connect gives them the product. A crooked NOPD detective gives them the protection. A Bourbon Street strip club gives them a laundromat. And a Gentilly recording studio gives Dre the one thing none of them have ever had — a way out.

Over one season — from Super Sunday in spring to a jazz funeral in winter — the four learn what every crew in this city learns eventually: the streets don't keep secrets, the cops don't keep promises, and the band always plays the same song at the end.

---

## THE CREW

**DASHON "LOCS" BATISTE (21)** — The leader. Waist-length dreads he's been growing since his father drowned in the Lower Ninth when the Industrial Canal floodwall broke during Hurricane Katrina; Locs was a baby in his mother's arms on a rooftop. Calm, strategic, reads everything. Believes he can run the game like a business and walk away clean. He's wrong about the second part.

**TERRELL "T-BONE" GUIDRY (21)** — The muscle and the heart. Loyal to a fault, quick to the trigger, funny as hell when he isn't angry. Carries a Draco — a pistol-length AK-47 — in a gym bag and treats a Glock like a phone. The first to shoot, the last to sleep.

**ANDRE "DRE FLY" MARIGNY (20)** — The rapper and producer. Freeform dreads dyed gold at the tips. Writes bars on receipts and makes beats on a cracked laptop. His bounce-meets-trap sound is the show's soundtrack — and his ticket out, if the streets let him go.

**KENDRICK "KEBO" LEBLANC (21)** — The money. A former honor student who lost a scholarship when his mother got sick. Counts cash in his grandmother's shotgun house, keeps the books in code, and says the least. The quietest one in the room — and the one carrying the heaviest secret.

### THE WOMEN WHO RUN THE CITY

**NIA "HONEY" ROBICHEAUX (24)** — Headliner at The Velvet Bayou on Bourbon Street. Curves that stop the room, a mind that runs it. Paying her way through nursing school at Delgado one night at a time. She knows where every dollar in the Quarter goes — and she's the smartest player in the series.

**ATF SPECIAL AGENT MONIQUE DUPRE (38)** — A New Orleans East native who came home with a federal badge. Patient, precise, relentless. She doesn't want the crew. She wants whoever's been selling them protection.

**MISS ODILE THERIOT (60s)** — Owner and engineer of Mud Bug Sound in Gentilly. Recorded bounce legends in the '90s. The closest thing Dre has to a mother in the music business.

**CELESTE BATISTE (40)** — Locs's mother. Works doubles at a hotel on Canal Street. Knows exactly what her son is doing and prays every night he'll stop.

### THE OPPOSITION

**DET. RAYMOND "RAY" THIBODEAUX (52)** — 25 years in NOPD. Charming, Catholic, deadly. Runs a private tax on half the dealers in the 7th Ward. He doesn't just protect the crew — he *owns* them.

**"SAINT" MOUTON (27)** — Leader of the Plank Road Bloods, a Baton Rouge set expanding into New Orleans. Red bandana, cold eyes, fiercely protective of his little brother, Jules. Not the villain he looks like.

**"BIG WICK" ALCORN (40s)** — Houston connect, Crip-affiliated, all blue everything. Calm, generous, and absolutely clear that a debt is a debt.

**LUCIEN FONTENOT (50s)** — Owner of The Velvet Bayou. Washes money for anyone who pays his percentage and believes he's untouchable.

## EPISODE GUIDE

### EPISODE 1 — "BRASS & BLOOD"

**COLD OPEN:** Super Sunday. A brass band roars under the Claiborne overpass. Mardi Gras Indians in hand-sewn feathered suits, tubas flashing gold, the crowd moving as one body. Locs, T-Bone, Dre and Kebo dance at the front with Locs's little cousin, **Jojo (15)**. Two cars pass. Pop-pop-pop. The crowd scatters like birds. When the smoke clears, Jojo is on the asphalt.

Jojo survives — but the shooters were Plank Road Bloods aiming at someone else, and nobody from the city calls Celeste back. In the hospital waiting room, Locs makes a decision: *never again.*

Honey, an old friend from St. Augustine High's football games, introduces Locs to Big Wick in the VIP of The Velvet Bayou. Wick fronts them two kilos of cocaine on one condition: pay on time, or don't come back.

On the drive home, the crew gets pulled over on St. Bernard Avenue. Det. Ray Thibodeaux finds T-Bone's Glock — and instead of cuffs, he offers a deal. He'll keep NOPD off their corners for 20% of everything. In return, he wants the Plank Road Bloods gone from the 7th Ward.

**CLOSING IMAGE:** Two bricks on the kitchen table of Kebo's grandmother's shotgun house, next to a framed picture of Jesus. T-Bone loads the Draco. Dre whispers his first bar into his phone. Locs stares at the money counter and says: *"We don't stop till we own the block."*

**MUSIC CUE:** "Under the Bridge" (Dre Fly) — brass sample over 808s.

---

### EPISODE 2 — "SHOTGUN HOUSE"

Kebo builds the operation like a business plan: three corners, phone rotations, cash logs disguised as grandma's church donation book. The money is real and fast. For the first time, Locs pays his mother's light bill — she doesn't ask where it came from.

Dre spends nights at Mud Bug Sound recording "Under the Bridge" with Miss Odile, who hears something special: *"You got that old bounce in you, baby. Don't let them streets take it."*

Saint's Bloods rob one of the crew's corners on Elysian Fields and pistol-whip a 17-year-old runner. T-Bone wants blood. Locs wants patience. Honey shows Locs how the Quarter really works — how Lucien Fontenot turns dirty cash into clean receipts — and plants the idea that the smartest move is to think like a businessman, not a soldier.

But T-Bone doesn't wait. He takes Kebo to drive.

**CLOSING IMAGE:** Midnight on St. Claude Avenue. T-Bone hangs out the window with the Draco. The rounds miss Saint completely and shred a parked car. Kebo drives home shaking, vomits in his grandmother's azaleas, and stares at his hands for the rest of the night.

**MUSIC CUE:** "Elysian" (Dre Fly) — slow, haunting piano loop.

---

### EPISODE 3 — "VELVET BAYOU"

Strip club episode. Neon pink and purple, money raining from the balcony. The lights drop, the DJ cuts to **"Pillow" (dblack4)** and Honey takes the main stage for her headline set. The track carries the whole sequence: the slow walk out under one pink spotlight, the room going quiet, then the balcony emptying stacks of ones as the beat lands. Locs watches from the VIP, and for the first time all season he isn't thinking about the block.

When Honey's set ends, the DJ follows "Pillow" with Dre's "Under the Bridge." A dancer's video of the crowd going crazy to it hits a million views overnight. Dre is suddenly somebody.

Locs cuts a deal with Lucien: the club washes their cash for 15%. Thibodeaux arrives uninvited, sits in the VIP and raises his tax to 30%. When Locs pushes back, Thibodeaux slides him a photo of Celeste leaving work. Message received.

Honey's B-story: we see her day life — anatomy exams, a son she's raising alone, a mother who thinks she works at a hotel. She and Locs grow close in the hours after the club closes, eating beignets at 5 a.m. Theirs is the most honest relationship in the show.

**CLOSING IMAGE:** ATF Special Agent Monique Dupre walks the St. Claude shooting scene with an NOPD crime-lab tech. Camera footage shows the rounds coming faster than any finger can pull a trigger. She picks up a shell casing and smiles: *"This just became a federal case."*

**MUSIC CUES:** "Pillow" (dblack4): Honey's main-stage set at The Velvet Bayou. "Under the Bridge" (Dre Fly): the DJ's follow-up that goes viral. "Pink Light" (Dre Fly feat. Honey): the 5 a.m. beignet scene with Locs.

---

### EPISODE 4 — "SWITCH"

T-Bone buys Glock "switches" — illegal auto-sears that turn a handgun into a fully automatic machine gun — from a kid selling them out of a car trunk in Algiers. He passes them out to the crew like party favors. Locs is furious: under federal law, a single switch counts as a machine gun and can carry up to ten years in federal prison. T-Bone shrugs: *"Feds don't come to the 7th Ward."*

Dupre is already building the case — tracing the switches, the casings, and the money. And she's noticed something: none of the crew's corners ever get raided. Somebody is protecting them.

Saint retaliates for St. Claude. His crew shoots up Mud Bug Sound in the middle of Dre's session. Miss Odile is hit in the shoulder shielding Dre. In the hospital, Dre tells Locs he's done — he's got a meeting with an Atlanta label. The crew's first real fracture: Locs and T-Bone fight in the parking lot, fists and tears.

**CLOSING IMAGE:** Kebo is snatched off Esplanade Avenue — not by the Bloods, but by Dupre. In a federal interview room, she lays out his future: conspiracy, machine-gun possession, ten to life. Or: help her get the cop. Kebo stares at the photo of Thibodeaux. Long silence. *"What do I gotta do?"*

**MUSIC CUE:** Silence. Then a single snare from a brass band, far away.

---

### EPISODE 5 — "HURRICANE SEASON"

A Category 3 storm, Hurricane Marisol, bears down on the Gulf. The city evacuates. Contraflow on I-10. Boarded windows on Magazine Street. For Big Wick, empty streets are opportunity: he sends ten kilos — the crew's biggest load ever.

Flashback (2005): Celeste on the roof of their Lower Ninth home with baby Dashon in her arms, watching the water rise over the place her husband had been. It's the origin of Locs's dreads, his distrust of the city, and his promise to never be helpless again.

Thibodeaux drafts Locs and T-Bone into robbing Saint's stash house in the storm — no witnesses, no cameras, no police response. But in the chaos, Thibodeaux shoots and kills **Jules Mouton (19)** — Saint's little brother — with T-Bone's switched Glock, which he lifted from the car. Then he keeps the gun.

**CLOSING IMAGE:** The hurricane passes. Power out across the city. Thibodeaux, alone in his cruiser, drops T-Bone's Glock into an evidence bag and labels it. He now owns a murder he can hang on T-Bone any time he wants.

**MUSIC CUE:** "Marisol" (Dre Fly) — rain recorded through a cracked window, a voice memo of Celeste singing gospel.

---

### EPISODE 6 — "ALL SAINTS"

November 1. All Saints' Day. Families whitewash the tombs in St. Roch Cemetery. Saint lays flowers on Jules's fresh grave and swears revenge on T-Bone, whose name is being whispered as the shooter.

T-Bone goes into hiding at a cousin's camp in Lafitte. Locs knows his friend didn't do it — but can't prove it. Honey can. She overheard Thibodeaux in the VIP bragging to Lucien about "storm money" and "a gun he's saving."

Dre gets the Atlanta offer: a real deal, a real advance, a flight in two weeks. He doesn't know whether to take it or stay and fight.

Saint's crew grabs Dre outside the Velvet Bayou. Locs walks into a Bloods stronghold on Plank Road in Baton Rouge alone, unarmed, dreads down — and tells Saint the truth: the man who killed Jules wears a badge.

**CLOSING IMAGE:** Saint and Locs, enemies, shaking hands in the headlights. *"You give me the cop,"* Saint says, *"and the war is over."*

**MUSIC CUE:** "All Saints" (Dre Fly) — a choir sample over a slow bounce rhythm.

---

### EPISODE 7 — "BOURBON & BURN"

The plan: Honey records Thibodeaux confessing in the VIP. Kebo, sweating through his shirt, finally confesses to Locs that he's been wearing an ATF wire for two months. Locs doesn't hit him. He hugs him — then uses it. The crew will feed Dupre everything on Thibodeaux.

Honey's VIP scene is the series' tensest 10 minutes: a slow dance, a hidden phone, Thibodeaux's hand on her waist, and his voice sliding into a confession about Jules — *"That boy was collateral. The city don't miss him."*

Thibodeaux finds out something is wrong. He confronts Lucien after close and kills him in the club's back office, then torches the books. The Velvet Bayou burns on Bourbon Street while tourists film it on their phones.

**CLOSING IMAGE:** T-Bone, out of hiding and done waiting, walks into a packed French Quarter crowd with a switched Glock, hunting Thibodeaux. Locs sprints through the crowd behind him, screaming his name.

**MUSIC CUE:** "Burn Bourbon" (Dre Fly) — distorted brass, siren samples.

---

### EPISODE 8 — "SECOND LINE"

Finale. In an alley off Dauphine Street, Locs reaches T-Bone a second too late: Thibodeaux shoots first. T-Bone dies in Locs's arms, still trying to make a joke.

Dupre moves. Honey's recording and Kebo's wire bring a federal arrest — Thibodeaux is taken out of his own precinct in handcuffs on live TV. The Plank Road war ends without another shot fired. Saint sends one red rose and one blue rose to T-Bone's funeral.

But the feds don't stop at the cop. Dupre's case includes ten kilos, the switches, and the money. Locs knows it's coming. He spends his last free day getting Dre to the airport, sitting with Jojo — who's learning to walk again — and kissing Honey goodbye on her front porch.

T-Bone's jazz funeral: the brass band plays the dirge slow down Orleans Avenue, then cuts loose. The second line dances. Dre stands on a car roof and raps "Under the Bridge" a cappella as the tuba picks up his beat. The whole 7th Ward sings along.

**FINAL SEQUENCE:** Federal agents arrest Locs at the edge of the second line. He doesn't run. He looks back at the crowd still dancing and smiles — because that's what this city does. Kebo watches from a car with federal plates, on his way to a new name in a new state. Honey walks across the stage at Delgado graduation.

**SEASON 2 HOOK:** In federal holding, Locs meets his new lawyer — expensive, polished, paid in full by an anonymous source. The lawyer slides a note across the table: *"Houston still owes you. And Captain Arceneaux says hello."* Thibodeaux's boss is still out there.

**MUSIC CUE:** "Second Line (For T-Bone)" (Dre Fly) — brass band, live crowd, no drums but the people's feet.

## THEMES

- **Brotherhood vs. survival.** Four friends who'd die for each other learn that the game makes that promise literal.
- **The system is the real cartel.** The most powerful dealer in the show has a badge, a pension and a parish.
- **Art as escape.** Dre's music is the only path out — and it only works because his friends paid for it.
- **The city that dances at funerals.** New Orleans turns grief into celebration. *Second Line* asks what that costs.

---

## SERIES CREATOR'S NOTE ON AUTHENTICITY

New Orleans street life is famously local. Crews are tied to wards, projects and blocks rather than national gang brands, which is why the Bloods and Crips in *Second Line* come from **outside** the city — Baton Rouge and Houston — and why the friends' loyalty is to the 7th Ward first. The show also reflects real federal law: Glock switches are treated as machine guns under the National Firearms Act, and that is what turns a local crew into a federal case.
